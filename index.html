01The poster

How do the climates of Melbourne and Singapore affect plant stress, and where air-quality sensors should go?

Leaves · John Monash Science School

  • Nasturtium leaves kept the same shape in Melbourne-like (52–54%) and Singapore-like (90–96%) humidity over 26 days.
  • Dry air caused a short shock in the first week (duller leaves, more leaves lost), but no lasting colour difference.

Sensors · NUS High School

  • Placing PM2.5 sensors with the weather in mind made pollution maps 10–50% more accurate when sources are predictable.
  • Melbourne didn't benefit more than Singapore: how densely sensors cover a city mattered more.

02Project timeline

  1. Term 2, 2026Proposal with NUS High. Our first plan was wider around smoke and haze, leaf pores and a laser beam. We narrowed it down to what we could test properly.
  2. 29 Jun – 16 AugPhase 1: field trips. Four visits to street trees at three EPA Victoria air-monitoring sites (Alphington, Footscray, Melbourne CBD). Leaves photographed at about 10× to count surface particles (ImageJ) and trace veins.
  3. AugustThe optical box. A light source shines across a box to a sensor while smoke burns in a dish between them, to see how much smoke and PM 2.5 Concentration disrupt the light.
  4. Why we changedFicus leaves are oval with no fixed centre, and the field photos had no scale. Nasturtium's round leaf has a central hub that every landmark can be measured from, so Phase 2 moved into the lab.
  5. 21 Sep – 16 OctPhase 2: two tanks, 26 days. A dry, Melbourne-like tank and a humid, Singapore-like tank, with every tagged leaf photographed daily on grid paper (263 photos).
  6. 18 OctTests written down first. Before comparing the Days 17–26 leaves, we wrote down exactly which tests we'd run, so the result couldn't be cherry-picked.
  7. Late OctoberLeaf Landmarks app built from our measuring code, and checked against 68 of our photos.
  8. In parallelNUS High: sensor simulations. Five years of weather and PM2.5 data, pollution simulated on 2 km grids, and sensor networks chosen for each city.
  9. NovemberJoint poster for ISSF 2026.

03Extra Results

Leaves · John Monash Science School

How reliable are our measurements?

  • Re-measuring the same photos changed leaf shape by only 0.012 (Procrustes distance), less than one leaf changes from one day to the next (0.030) and far less than two leaves differ (0.063).
  • Scales read automatically from the grid paper were within 1.35% of careful hand measurements (median of 89 photos).
  • The smallest differences our Days 17–26 test could reliably detect were 0.032 in greenness, 1.2% per day in growth and 0.029 in shape. "No difference" means no difference bigger than these.

04Takeaways and next steps

What we'd do differently

  • Control temperature. The humid tank also ran hotter (up to 48 °C vs 39 °C), so heat and humidity were mixed up.
  • More tanks. With one tank per condition, a difference between tanks can't be pinned on humidity alone.
  • Tag more leaves at the start. Replacing dying leaves (4 of 5 in the dry tank) meant few leaves were followed all the way through.
  • Beat the glare. 39 of 263 leaf outlines had to be traced by hand because of glare or brown edges.
  • Scale in every photo. Without a scale marker, the Phase 1 field photos could only be compared in pixels.

Next steps

  • Leaves: a temperature-controlled trial at the same two humidities, and young, still-growing leaves, whose shape may respond more.
  • Sensors: finer 0.5 km grids and finer wind data, especially for Singapore.

05Leaf Landmarks app

Our measuring method as a free Windows app: click a leaf's vein hub, and it finds the outline, places the 13 landmarks, reads the scale from the grid paper and measures size, shape and greenness.

For nasturtium leaves only (for now). You click the vein hub yourself on every photo; everything after that click is automatic.
